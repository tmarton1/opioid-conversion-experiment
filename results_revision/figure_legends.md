# Draft legends: example figures E1–E4

Built by `python -m visualize.revision.examples_scores` from `results_revision/responses_long.csv`; files in `summary_w_figures/revision/examples/`. Models are in the fixed global order and colors used by every figure (never by rank). Scores: Nielsen (the original reference); all standards (nearest of Nielsen, CDC 2022, NIH HEAL, McPherson 2018); all except Nielsen (nearest of the three non-Nielsen standards; conversions only Nielsen covers are excluded, so it scores 19 of 20 vignette scenarios and 40 of 49 random-dose pairs).

## E1. Naive vignette accuracy under three scores, bands and distribution views (`E1_fig01_naive_vignettes_score_view_grid`)
Figure 1 rebuilt as a grid: columns are the three scores, rows are the two views. Top row (bands view): percentage of each model's naive text-vignette responses within the tight (0.85–1.15×, darker bar) and broad (0.6–1.7×, lighter bar) bands, averaged over scenarios as in the CMH rankings. Bottom row (distribution view): every response's observed / reference dose on a log fold axis (violin plus up to 250 responses per model), black tick = median, tight and broad bands shaded; right margin gives median fold error (MFE), % of responses above 2× and % below ½×. n = responses and scenarios scored under each score.

## E2a / E2b. Reference-grounding effect under three scores, tight and broad bands (`E2a_fig11_grounding_vignettes_score_view_grid`, `E2b_fig11_grounding_random_doses_score_view_grid`)
Figure 11 (naive vs Nielsen factors supplied) for text vignettes (E2a) and random doses (E2b); columns are the three scores. Rows 1–2 (bands view): each model's batch-mean percentage of responses within the band, naive (faint circle) and grounded (square), one row per band: tight (0.85–1.15×) and broad (0.6–1.7×). The line under each panel title gives the mean across models, naive → grounded. Bottom row (distribution view): median (marker), interquartile range (thick bar) and 10th–90th percentile range (thin line) of observed / reference, naive above grounded, with each model's median fold error naive → grounded at right. Because the tight band (1.15-fold) is narrower than the typical disagreement between published standards, a model that copies the supplied Nielsen factors can fall outside tight under "all except Nielsen" by construction; broad (0.6–1.7×), just wider than the largest disagreement between current standards (1.5-fold), and median fold error are the fair tests of circularity. Safety tails (overdose, severe overdose, underdose, in dose terms against the current standards) are reported in tables and in E8.

## E3. Ranking shifts across scores (`E3_ranking_shifts_three_scores`)
Slope chart of each model's rank (1 = best) under Nielsen, all standards and all except Nielsen, naive condition; text vignettes (left) and random doses (right). Top row: rank by mean tight accuracy, the sort key of the published CMH ranking; the number in each marker is the CMH adjusted rank (significance tier, Bonferroni-corrected), so models sharing a number are not significantly different. Bottom row: rank by median fold error, a threshold-free measure (tied models share the average rank; labels are spread to separate slots with leader lines). Colors follow the model. Data: `E3_ranking_shifts_data.csv`.

## E4. Tolerance curves under three scores (`E4_tolerance_curves_three_scores`)
Percentage of each model's naive responses whose fold error (max(r, 1/r), r = observed / reference) is at or below each tolerance from 1× to 4× (log axis); columns are the three scores, rows text vignettes and random doses. Unusable answers count as outside every tolerance. Shading and solid lines mark tight (fold error ≤ 1.15, i.e. 0.87–1.15×, slightly narrower than the 0.85–1.15× band) and broad (≤ 1.7, i.e. 0.59–1.7×). The note gives the median and maximum spread between standards (per conversion; highest / lowest current standard) for that experiment's scored conversions. A curve that rises earlier is more accurate at every tolerance; curves that cross mean the ranking depends on the threshold.

# Draft figure legends: example figures E5-E7

Generated by `python -m visualize.revision.examples_references`; every number is computed from the CSVs.

## E5. Reference standards compared, by agent (`E5_reference_standards_by_agent.png`)

Oral morphine equivalent factor of each study agent under each published standard, relative to the Nielsen median (log scale), for the 14 of 19 study agents other than oral morphine that at least one alternative standard covers. Grey bars: Nielsen interquartile range; shading: tight (0.85–1.15×) and broad (0.6–1.7×) bands. 10 of 14 agents have at least one standard outside the tight band (8 excluding the superseded CDC 2016 factors, open circles); outside the broad band: Tramadol (oral) (CDC 2016 only). Nielsen is the lone outlier (all current alternatives agree with each other and differ from Nielsen) for Codeine (oral), Hydrocodone (oral), Fentanyl (patch). No alternative standard: Buprenorphine (IM), Buprenorphine (IV), Fentanyl (SC), Methadone (IV), Nalbuphine (parenteral). Model-independent.

## E6. Agreement between models (`E6_model_agreement_spread.png`)

One point per conversion (naive condition, Nielsen score, 13 models). x: within-model spread (median over models of the SD of the log ratio); y: between-model spread (SD of the 13 model medians), both plotted as exp(SD) on log axes; dashed line: identity. Open diamonds: the spread between the current standards for the same conversion (max/min fold), joined to the model point by a grey line. Random-dose pairs: between > within for 48 of 49; between-model spread exceeds the between-standard range for 36 of 40 pairs with an alternative standard (medians: within 1.41×, between models 1.92×, between standards 1.18×). Text vignettes: 20 of 20 and 18 of 19 (medians 1.17×, 1.65×, 1.2×). The vignette within-model spread pools each scenario's four wordings. Comparing a range (standards) with an SD (models) favors the standards.

## E7. Worked scoring examples (`E7_scoring_examples.png`)

For four conversions to oral morphine, the fold error each score assigns to every possible answer (curves; x = answer relative to the Nielsen expected dose, log scale) and the actual naive random-dose responses of the 13 models (histograms). Markers: the expected dose under each current standard; shading: tight and broad bands on the assigned error. Under the all-standards and all-except-Nielsen scores, an answer matching any standard in the set scores 1×; otherwise its error is the fold distance to the nearest. Responses within the tight band: oral hydrocodone → oral morphine (n = 554): Nielsen 3%, all standards 51%, all except Nielsen 48%; IV hydromorphone → oral morphine (n = 335): Nielsen 38%, all standards 40%, all except Nielsen 1%; oral oxycodone → oral morphine (n = 433): Nielsen 85%, all standards 86%, all except Nielsen 86%; oral codeine → oral morphine (n = 555): Nielsen 46%, all standards 78%, all except Nielsen 32%. Unusable answers count as outside every band.

## E8. Overdose exceedance by condition (`E8_overdose_exceedance_by_condition`)
Percentage of responses (13 models pooled) whose dose exceeds each multiple, from 1× to 10× (log axis), of the highest current standard for that conversion (Nielsen, CDC 2022, NIH HEAL, McPherson 2018). Safety is defined in dose terms against the standards, so the curve is the same under every score. Panels: text vignettes (naive, Nielsen supplied, Nielsen plus unit explanation), random doses in mg/day (naive, Nielsen supplied) and the dosing-unit experiment in mg/4 h (Nielsen supplied, units not explained vs explained). Dashed vertical lines mark overdose (> 2×) and severe overdose (> 4×). Per-model values: `results_revision/safety_exceedance.csv`; the count of responses at or above 10× is given in the text, not plotted. Built by `python -m visualize.revision.examples_safety`.
